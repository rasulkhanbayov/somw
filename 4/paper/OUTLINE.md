## The claim (honest version, to agree with the professor)

**Updated Oct 19 after Phase 9 (confirmed; `results/p9_table/SUMMARY.md`):** ours is the only pipeline within risk 0.03 on all three fresh test sets below the cost of exact simulation: in distribution 0.020 at 0.26×, under shift 0.013 at 0.71× (the cheapest safe pipeline; every tuned simulator 0.10–0.16), contact 0.012 at 0.90×. Where the regime is known in advance a tuned simulator is cheaper (M1 in distribution 0.13×; M3 on contact 0.50×, M1 0.029 at 0.13×). On contact the shadow-timed switch buys accuracy (error 0.015 vs 0.029), not risk: chain timing reaches 0.012 at 0.67×. Main table T1 = `tab/main_table.tex`, teaser candidate = `fig/tradeoff.pdf` (both generated by `code/p9_table.py`). The paragraphs below are the earlier (Oct 17) framing, kept for history.

A trajectory generator (PhysCtrl) is fast but fails silently under distribution shift and contact; its own uncertainty does not detect the failures. We verify a generated trajectory with a few short exact-simulator windows restarted from states lifted out of it, turn the window defect into a calibrated accept/reject certificate, and repair a rejected trajectory by re-simulating only its uncertified suffix (from a cheap shadow's full state; at contacts, the switch is timed by the shadow). **Result: the only pipeline that stays safe on every benchmark (risk ≤ 0.025), at about half the cost of exact simulation in distribution; under shift and contact it is safe at roughly the cost of simulating, which the cheap shortcuts are not.**

Also from 7a (Oct 17): a simulator-free momentum-residual gate is **as safe** as ours under shift (0.018 vs 0.015; Q3 FAIL) but costs 0.79× vs 0.44× in distribution, so the claim is "cheapest safe pipeline", not "safest". Our window score is the best ranker of generation error (AUROC 0.977 pooled vs residual 0.886, seed spread 0.671; Q1, Q2 PASS); best-of-8 cannot fix shift (even the oracle has risk 0.676; Q4 PASS). At ε = 0.05 (stricter than calibrated) ours is worse than coarse only in distribution.

From 7c (Oct 17), every gate recalibrated per target: **under shift ours is the cheapest pipeline meeting the target in all six cells with ε ∈ {0.05, 0.10}**; in distribution only at (ε 0.10, α 0.05). At the strict ε = 0.05 in distribution ours misses the target (0.12 vs α 0.10; S1, S3 FAIL) — the coarse-shadow repair has an error floor near 0.05; a full exact fallback is the safe choice there. State the ε dependence in the main text; full grid in the supplement.

Not claimed: beating always-simulate on cost under hard shift or contact (0.94–1.05×); generative repair (failed six ways); event-aware window *placement* (failed); large-model benefit (seed spread suffices there).

## Section plan

### Abstract (≈ 180 words)
Problem (silent failures of learned trajectory priors) → method (sparse exact probes, conformal certificate, suffix repair, shadow-timed fidelity switch) → evidence (pre-registered, fresh data): certificate lets through 1.2% of failures under shift vs 35% for seed spread (4m); repair at 0.53× exact cost with risk 0.025 in distribution (4k); contact risk 0.012 (4o) → honest limit (under hard shift and contact the cost approaches exact simulation).

### 1 Introduction
1. Physics-grounded video generation drives a renderer with a generated 3D trajectory (PhysCtrl). Cheap, but wrong when the query leaves the training range or objects collide; the error is silent (F5: the earphone looks plausible, moves wrongly).
2. Two existing answers and their failure: trust the prior (risk 0.70 under shift, 0.75 on contact; 4m, 4o) or simulate everything (exact but 20× generation; Phase 1). Cheap middle grounds break under shift: coarse simulation 0.374, seed-spread deferral 0.242 (4m).
3. Our idea in one paragraph + F1.
4. Contributions:
   - (C1) a residual-free, object-level conformal certificate from sparse exact windows; valid (0.084 ≤ 0.11; 4a ext), robust under shift (1.2% vs 35%; 4m).
   - (C2) certificate-gated repair that re-simulates only the uncertified suffix, with event-aware fidelity at contacts (switch timed by a cheap shadow); 0.53× in distribution (4k), contact risk 0.012 and 0.65 s/query saved vs certify-or-simulate (4o).
   - (C3) compute-accounted evaluation with pre-registered tests on fresh data, new shift and two-object contact benchmarks, and audited amortisation of the verifier (small: −15% probe calls; 5a).
5. One honest sentence on scope (elastic, PhysCtrl base; savings in distribution).

### 2 Related work
- Physics-grounded video generation: PhysCtrl, simulate-then-render (PSIVG, RealWonder) \CITE{2603.06408, 2603.05449}.
- Physics benchmarks: Physics-IQ, VideoPhy-2, RigidBench \CITE{2501.09038, 2503.06800, 2608.15555}; we score 3D trajectories against the simulator (visual quality does not track physics).
- Hybrid surrogates and trust gating: Hybrid Neural World Models, ANCHOR, HyPER, NeuroForge, TACR \CITE{2605.28317, 2512.19643, 2503.10048, 2607.10333, 2610.02487}; positioning sentences 1–2 in the literature note.
- Conformal / selective prediction: split conformal, hierarchical conformal (Dunn et al.), conformal cascades, adaptive conformal \CITE{...}.
- Parareal-style coarse/fine coupling \CITE{2405.01355}: one verification-gated restart, not iteration.
- Inference-time constraint/imputation for diffusion (CondMDI, PCFM) \CITE{2405.11126, 2506.04171}: consistent with our failed generative repair.

### 3 Method
3.1 Problem and risk (query, generator, exact simulator, error, risk = P(error > ε), ε = 0.10 ≈ 5% of object size).
3.2 Window probes: lift positions + velocities from generated frames, F = I (`lift_noF`; why: F-lifting blew up the simulator, Phase 2); CFL-adaptive coarse fidelity (1/8 cost, 2c A′); m = 4 frames; placement `spread_event`; blow-up = +∞.
3.3 Certificate: score = max window defect; object-level split conformal threshold τ (replicate-0 rule), statement of the guarantee under object exchangeability; τ = 0.02614.
3.4 Repair: why not generative (verification ≠ correction: a restart from generated states removes 20% of error vs 94% from the true start; Goodhart in 4c). Gated chain: accept by the certificate; on rejection, the first failing chain window picks k; the reference suffix restarts from a coarse shadow's full state (positions, velocities, F, C) at k; anchor failure → full reference.
3.5 Event-aware fidelity at contact: the shadow (true start) detects approach within 3 coarse cells; restart one frame earlier; why the generation's own timing fails (4n).
3.6 Amortised verification: learned skip rule + 10% random audits + split conformal on residuals; stationary-stream guarantee.
3.7 Cost accounting: A100 seconds; generation 0.236 s, reference 4.666 s; probes by fit; suffix by share; shadow by rate.

### 4 Experimental setup
- Data: PhysCtrl's released test set (100), calibration pool (1,200), fresh in-distribution (800 + 200), fresh shift (398: softer, stiffer, force 1.5–4×, drop), two-object contact (270 contact + 76 controls), PhysCtrl's 5 image examples (videos).
- Protocol: every test pre-registered before its data existed; thresholds never re-fit; object-cluster bootstrap.
- Baselines: generate only, coarse only, seed-spread deferral, accept-coarse-if-agrees (XF), momentum-residual gate (7a), best-of-8 (7a), certify-or-simulate, always simulate; ablations: uniform windows, shuffled restart timing, generation-timed switch.
- Metrics: risk, mean/tail/last-frame error, AURC/AUROC (7a), cost; PhysCtrl's MSE and DeformLoss (7b).

### 6 Limitations and conclusion
Elastic only; base model only (large: seed spread already works); cost ≈ exact under hard shift/contact; conservative certificate (rejects ~51% in distribution while 14% are bad; 41% of good contact controls); amortisation needs stationarity; no video-level metric study.
