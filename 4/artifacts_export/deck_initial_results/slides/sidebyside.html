Ours is the only method within the bound on all three benchmarks at less than the cost of exact simulation. Under shift it is the cheapest safe method, and every fixed simulator setting fails. Where the regime is known in advance, a tuned simulator is cheaper: M1 in distribution, M3 on contact.

Phase 9, pre-registered, all 7 tests pass: fresh data generated after the method and tests were fixed. 200 in-distribution queries, 400 shifted queries (4 families), 132 two-object contact scenes. Each object counts once.

"Not run": seed spread and the residual gate were not part of the Phase 9 contact test. On the earlier contact set (Phase 7d, 4o scenes) the residual gate had risk 0.290 (a collision does not change total momentum, so it cannot see an ignored contact) and seed spread 0.086 at 1.66×. Confidence intervals (99%): in distribution ours costs 0.26× [0.21, 0.31] and saves 1.30 s/query vs certify-or-simulate; under shift ours saves 0.76 s [0.62, 0.91] and is 0.090 [0.055, 0.128] less risky than always-M1. On contact, restarting at the first failing chain window reaches the same risk (0.012) at 0.67× but with twice the mean error (0.029 vs 0.015); the shadow-timed switch buys accuracy. Under shift the more accurate M3 is wrong more often than M1 on very soft and strongly pushed objects, which suggests step-size sensitivity there; ground-truth convergence was not tested. Ours here = one-window certificate + medium-fidelity repair (single objects); three-window certificate + M3 repair at the shadow-timed switch (contact).
