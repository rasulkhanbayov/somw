Limits a reviewer will raise

Narrow scope so far

Elastic objects and PhysCtrl's base model (now also two-object contact). For the large model, seed spread already predicts error.

A tuned simulator is cheaper where its regime is known

In distribution M1 reaches risk 0.015 at 0.13× (ours 0.26×); on contact M3 reaches 0.000 at 0.50× (ours 0.90×). Both fail under shift (0.10–0.16).

The certificate has blind spots

It is conservative on contact (accepts 1 of 45 good generations) and leaks for very stiff materials (exploratory: 6 of 16 bad accepted at E ≥ 10⁸ Pa).

Small or missing effects

Event-aware window placement failed on contacts; amortisation saves only 1.3%; restart timing adds 0.002 error.

Phase 9 (fresh, pre-registered) costs: 0.26× in distribution, 0.71× under shift, 0.90× on contact. Earlier version (exact repair suffix): under hard shift the certified pipelines cost about as much as always simulating (up to 1.14× on the drop split, because the probes add overhead), which the medium-fidelity repair has since brought down to 0.71× pooled over the four shifts. Contact benchmark (Phases 4l, 4l-ref, 4n, 4o): placing windows at the contact did not help (H1–H3 failed), because the coarse grid cannot resolve the contact gap. What did help is switching fidelity there: restart at the exact setting just before the coarse simulation's objects come within 3 grid cells. The generation-timed version narrowly missed its risk margin (0.034 vs 0.030); the shadow-timed version was confirmed (risk 0.012). Amortisation (Phase 5a) was confirmed but small: probe rate 0.99 → 0.85, and on the shifted part of a drifting stream the skipped queries had risk 0.048.
