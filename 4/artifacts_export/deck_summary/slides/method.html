Our idea: generate once, simulate only what fails

1 · Generate

PhysCtrl predicts the motion, fast.

2 · Check

Run the simulator for a short window from the predicted motion. If they drift apart, reject.

3 · Repair

Keep the part that passed; re-simulate only the rest, from a cheap simulation of the true start.

4 · Collisions

The cheap simulation sees objects approaching and hands over to an accurate setting just before they touch.

Our three contributions

A trustworthy check

Short simulator windows turn into an accept/reject certificate with a guaranteed cap on bad motions it lets through.

Repair only what failed

Re-simulate just the unchecked part, and switch to a collision-ready setting at the right moment.

An honest test

Every test fixed before its data existed; new benchmarks for unfamiliar queries and collisions; compared with tuned simulators.

Step 2 in more detail: a “window” restarts the physics simulator from a state read off the generated frames and runs it for 4 frames. If the generated motion were physically right, the simulator would follow it; how far they drift apart is the score. A threshold calibrated on separate objects (split conformal, object level) turns the score into accept/reject with a finite-sample bound on accepted failures. Step 3: the generated frames do not contain the object’s internal stress, so the repair starts from a cheap “shadow” simulation of the true initial state, which does. Step 4: at collisions the cheap grid cannot resolve the gap between objects, so the shadow detects when they come within 3 grid cells and the repair switches to a finer setting one frame earlier. Tried and failed: repairing with the generator itself (five attempts, at most 1% better), and placing check windows at the collision.
