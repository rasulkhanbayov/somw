With a 3× stronger push, ours follows the simulator

PhysCtrl’s five example images. Rows: PhysCtrl · ours · the physics simulator. Our check rejected all five and the repair cut the error 1.6–22×.

One middle frame per clip; errors are averaged over the whole clip. Earlier version of our pipeline (exact repair); the paper re-renders these with the final one.

What to look at: the middle row (ours, blue frame) should match the bottom row (the simulator) more closely than the top row (PhysCtrl) does. Clearest: the penguin, which raises its flipper in the simulation and in ours but not in PhysCtrl; the giraffe’s pose and the blanket’s shape also follow the simulation better. Caveats: (1) one frame can mislead: the earphone looks tidier in PhysCtrl at this frame, but its motion over the whole clip is the most wrong (0.222 vs ours 0.010); (2) only the earphone crosses the 0.10 failure line; the other four PhysCtrl clips drift (0.061–0.089) rather than fail outright; (3) this is the earlier pipeline (repair with the full simulator); the paper version is being re-rendered with the final, cheaper pipeline. “The simulator” is PhysCtrl’s own physics engine, the one its model was trained to imitate; it is not checked against real-world motion.
