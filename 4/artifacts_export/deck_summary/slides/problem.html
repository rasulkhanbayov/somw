Simulate Only What Matters · CVPR 2027

AI-generated motion looks right but is often physically wrong, and nothing warns you

PhysCtrl makes physics-based videos in two steps: a learned model predicts how the object’s 3D points move under a push, then a video model renders that motion. The motion model is fast, but outside its training conditions it is often wrong.

68%

of PhysCtrl’s motions are wrong when the material or push is outside its training range

69%

are wrong when two objects collide: it moves each object as if it were alone

20×

the cost of generating, if you run the physics simulator on every query instead

“Wrong” = average point error above 0.10. Fresh, pre-registered test data (Phase 9). Cost measured on an A100.

The problem in one sentence: a learned motion model is fast but silently wrong when the query is unfamiliar (new material, stronger push, gravity drop) or when objects collide; the model’s own uncertainty does not flag it (it accepted 99% of failed drops in our shift test). The obvious fixes both have a catch: simulating every query is about 20 times the cost of generating, and a cheaper tuned simulator works on familiar queries but is silently wrong on 10–16% of unfamiliar ones. You cannot know in advance which kind a query is. Numbers: Phase 9 (fresh data, pre-registered): generate-only risk 0.680 under shift, 0.693 on contact; cost ratio from Phase 1 (4.666 s vs 0.236 s).
