Tuned simulators win where you already know the query is familiar, but fail silently on unfamiliar ones. Ours never leaves the safe zone.

How to read it: look for the cheapest row that has no orange in any column. PhysCtrl alone and the coarse simulator are cheap but often wrong. The tuned simulators (cheaper settings of the same physics engine, chosen on known data) are excellent on familiar queries and collisions, but wrong on 10–16% of unfamiliar queries, and you cannot tell in advance which queries are unfamiliar. The momentum check is safe on single objects but costs nearly as much as simulating, and on an earlier collision test set it missed 29% (a collision does not change total momentum, so it cannot see an ignored collision). Ours: 2% at a quarter of the cost on familiar queries; 1.3% at 0.71× on unfamiliar queries, the cheapest safe option there; 1.2% at 0.90× on collisions. Unfamiliar = softer or stiffer material than training, a push 1.5–4× stronger than any in training, or a drop under gravity onto a floor (never seen in training). Pre-registered confidence intervals: in distribution ours costs 0.26× [0.21, 0.31]; under shift it saves 0.76 s per query vs check-else-simulate [0.62, 0.91] and is 9 points safer than M1 [5.5, 12.8].
